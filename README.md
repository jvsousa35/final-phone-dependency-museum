## Museum Topic

My museum topic is **phone dependency**, presented through a digital exhibit called **The Museum of Digital Attention** with the subtitle **The Attention Trap**.

The site explores how smartphones changed from useful communication tools into systems that shape attention, habits, emotions, routines, sleep, focus, and social behavior.

The goal of this museum is not to argue that phones are evil. The goal is to help visitors understand how phone dependency forms and how attention can be protected.

---

## Main Story / Guided Experience

The main story of the museum is that the phone started as a helpful tool, but slowly became an attention environment. The site guides visitors through how that shift happened and how it affects daily life.

The museum is organized as a guided route:

1. **Entrance**  
   Introduces the museum, the topic, and the idea of the attention trap.

2. **Exhibit 01: From Tool to Companion**  
   Shows how the phone moved from a communication device into an always-present part of daily life.

3. **Exhibit 02: The Attention Economy**  
   Explains how notifications, feeds, infinite scroll, and social feedback are designed to compete for attention.

4. **Exhibit 03: The Habit Loop**  
   Explains how checking behavior becomes automatic through cues, rewards, and repetition.

5. **Exhibit 04: The Human Cost**  
   Shows how phone dependency can affect focus, sleep, school/work, relationships, and mental load.

6. **Final Exhibit: The Attention Reset**  
   Gives visitors a reflection-based ending where they can make one small commitment to protect their attention.

7. **Process Page**  
   Explains the design choices, AI orchestration process, research, planning, and revision process.

8. **Sources Page**  
   Lists the sources used to support the exhibit.

## Design Style

The design style is **Modern Museum / Editorial**.

This style shaped the site through:

- large editorial typography
- exhibit labels and museum-style captions
- strong spacing
- a cream paper background
- dark ink-like text
- muted red and blue accents
- structured cards
- visual artifacts
- clean navigation
- a serious but reflective layout

I chose this style because the project needed to feel like a curated digital exhibit, not a normal informational website or random set of pages.

---

## Brand Archetype

The brand archetype is **The Sage**.

The Sage archetype fits this museum because the purpose is to teach, explain, and help visitors understand their own behavior. The tone is reflective, serious, and educational. It does not try to scare the visitor. Instead, it guides them through the topic and helps them think more clearly about their relationship with their phone.

---

## Cialdini Principle

The Cialdini principle used is **Commitment and Consistency**.

The site uses small reflection prompts throughout the exhibit. Visitors are asked to notice their own habits before they reach the final exhibit. At the end, they are invited to make one small attention reset commitment.

This principle works because people are more likely to follow through when they make a small, clear commitment that connects to something they already recognized about themselves.

## AI Orchestration Process

AI was used intentionally as part of the project workflow. I did not use one single prompt to generate the entire project. I used AI in stages and made decisions throughout the process.

I used AI to help:

- break down the assignment requirements
- choose and refine the museum topic
- plan the guided exhibit route
- organize the site structure
- develop the museum title and subtitle
- research phone dependency and attention
- create page concepts
- draft and revise exhibit writing
- choose the design style
- connect the design style to the topic
- identify the brand archetype
- select the Cialdini principle
- generate HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and README content
- review and improve the site so it felt more like a final-level museum experience

The final decisions, topic direction, tone, structure, and design choices were directed by me. AI supported the process, but I managed and revised the output.

## Research and Planning

Before building the final version, I planned the museum around a clear visitor journey. I wanted the site to feel like a guided exhibit instead of a basic report.

The research and planning focused on:

- how smartphones became central to daily life
- how apps compete for attention
- how notifications and infinite scroll affect behavior
- how checking habits are formed
- how phone dependency connects to sleep, focus, school/work, and relationships
- how visitors could reflect on their own habits
- how the museum could end with a useful attention reset

The structure was planned so the visitor moves from understanding the history of the phone, to recognizing the attention system, to reflecting on their own habits.
